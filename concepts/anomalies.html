Concepts

Anomalies

An anomaly is a named signal, not a verdict. Velocity spikes, amount deviations, duplicate references and timing patterns are compared with the behavior they came from, then routed to a review decision with context and evidence.

  • Signals are named — each flag states the pattern behind it: amount outside the typical range, N transactions from one account in M minutes, a reference seen before.
  • Context beats thresholds — a large payment is not a problem on its own; it is scored against the account and channel behaviour.
  • Review stays with people — a flag queues a transaction with severity and reason. PesaGuard does not auto-declare fraud, and blocking, if any, is your recorded decision.

Signal anatomy

FieldPurpose
Signal nameStates the recognizable pattern, such as velocity spike or duplicate reference.
SeverityPrioritizes review without claiming fraud.
Evidence windowDefines the account, channel, time range or comparison population.
Observed valuesShows the measurements that caused the signal.
DecisionRecords the human response and reason.

Review workflow

  1. Ingest the event and preserve its identity.
  2. Evaluate explicit rules and statistical checks.
  3. Attach the signal to the transaction and tenant.
  4. Notify the configured owner with delivery evidence.
  5. Review, resolve or escalate with a written decision.

Tuning without hiding risk

Measure false positives, review age, signal recurrence and confirmed outcomes before changing thresholds. A threshold change must be auditable and evaluated against historical traffic; disabling a noisy signal without recording the reason creates an evidence gap.

Today this is explicit rules plus statistical checks. These pages describe it that way rather than implying a machine-learning model that has not shipped.
