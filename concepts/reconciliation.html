Concepts

Reconciliation

Reconciliation compares an external provider event with the internal transaction it should represent. PesaGuard uses explicit evidence such as amount, reference and a configured timestamp tolerance, then records a deterministic outcome that can be reviewed months later.

  • Match keys: amount, reference and timestamp tolerance, configured per flow. Tolerance is a setting, not a hidden constant, because provider and ledger clocks rarely agree to the second.
  • Differences are first-class: a KES 500 amount mismatch is not smoothed over; it becomes an exception with the delta stated.
  • Exceptions carry reasons: unmatched, amount-mismatch and reference-not-found each raise with its own reason attached.
  • Safe to re-run: idempotency at the write means replays and provider retries never double-count.

Matching sequence

  1. Validate the provider payload and establish its event identity.
  2. Resolve the authorized tenant and locate candidate internal records.
  3. Compare reference, amount, currency and time-window evidence.
  4. Persist a matched, unmatched or exception outcome with reasons.
  5. Publish downstream notifications and expose the result through APIs and reports.

Outcome model

OutcomeWhat it provesNext action
MatchedEvidence satisfies the configured rules.Continue downstream processing and reporting.
UnmatchedNo valid internal candidate was found.Queue for investigation or await a late record.
ExceptionA candidate exists but evidence differs.Review the delta and record a decision.
DuplicateThe logical event was already accepted.Return the original durable outcome.

What happens to an exception

Exceptions queue with the transaction, the provider event and the reason. A reviewer resolves with a written decision, and the outcome lands in the append-only trail.

  1. Queue ordered by severity and age.
  2. Reviewer accepts, rejects or escalates: with a reason.
  3. Outcome recorded; reports read the reconciled result, not a parallel export.

What reconciliation must not do

  • Silently round or coerce an amount to force a match.
  • Use a client-supplied tenant id to search outside authorized scope.
  • Overwrite the original provider event or reviewer decision.
  • Turn a transient processing failure into a financial success.
